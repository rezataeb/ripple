[home]
Hi, I'm Reza, and this is Ripple, my project for the AI Engineer Hackathon NYC.

[home — verdict + fan chart visible]
Most pricing decisions come down to a single number that nobody really believes. Ripple gives you the whole range instead.

[home — preset "Student coupon in Boston"]
Here's a twenty percent student coupon in Boston. It adds about eight thousand four hundred dollars, and it's very likely to pay off.

[home — drag discount 10% to 30%]
I can drag the discount and everything moves right away. The model's already in the browser, so there's no waiting.

[home — point at the dip after the promo weeks]
Look at what happens after the promo ends. Demand dips, because a discount borrows sales from next week. If you ignore that, every promotion looks better than it really was.

[/launch — Austin launch, slider at 0]
Now the hard part. We've just opened in Austin, and we don't know yet if it was a good idea.

[/launch — drag the week slider 0 to 8]
Before any data, Ripple gives it a twenty-five percent chance of paying off. So, probably not. But then the real weeks start coming in.

[/launch — truth toggle on]
By week four it's up to ninety-four percent. By week eight it's basically certain. The forecast was wrong, and the data fixed it.

[/launch — gate badge]
And when a launch is too strange for the quick update, Ripple says so instead of guessing. That's what this badge is for.

[cmd-K — "Is Miami a good next city?"]
I can also just ask. It builds the scenario, runs the model, and checks what competitors are charging.

[Ask panel — extrapolation warning + market sources]
Miami sits outside anything we've seen, and it tells me that up front. Every number here came from a tool, not from the language model.

[/data — bring your own data]
Your own spreadsheet goes through exactly the same path. Ripple maps the columns, checks them, and fits the same model.

[/method — recovery + calibration charts]
I built the demo data myself, so I know the true answers. The model found all thirty of them.

[/method — stress arm on the calibration chart]
And I ran two hundred fake launches to see if the intervals hold up. They do. There's one case where they don't, and I put that on the page too.

[end]
The model makes the numbers. The agent explains them. The web gives context. Thanks for watching.
