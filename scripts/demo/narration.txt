[home]
Hi, I'm Reza, and this is Ripple, my project for the AI Engineer Hackathon NYC.

[home — verdict + fan chart visible]
Ripple answers "what happens to sales if" with a distribution, never a single number.

[home — preset "Student coupon in Boston"]
A twenty percent student coupon in Boston: incremental profit, a ninety percent interval, and the chance it pays.

[home — drag discount 10% to 30%]
Drag the discount and everything moves at once, because the whole posterior is in the browser.

[home — point at the dip after the promo weeks]
Notice the dip after the promotion. A promo borrows sales from next week, and the model charges it back.

[/launch — Austin launch, slider at 0]
Then reality arrives. Here is the Austin launch before any data.

[/launch — drag the week slider 0 to 8]
Each real week updates the forecast with Bayes' rule. The band narrows, and the chance it pays updates.

[/launch — truth toggle on]
The data was generated with a known answer, so I can show you the truth. It stays inside the interval the whole way.

[/launch — gate badge]
And when the launch is too surprising for the fast update, this badge says "refit needed" instead of pretending.

[cmd-K — "Is Miami a good next city?"]
The agent turns the question into a scenario, runs the model, and checks the live market.

[Ask panel — extrapolation warning + market sources]
Miami is outside the training range, and Ripple says so. Every number comes from a tool, with sources.

[/method — recovery + calibration charts]
Thirty of thirty planted values recovered. Six of six trust checks pass.

[/method — stress arm on the calibration chart]
And two hundred simulated launches say the intervals are honest, including the one case where they are not.

[end]
The model makes the numbers. The agent explains them. The web gives context. Thanks for watching.
